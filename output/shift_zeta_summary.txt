Ratio at the classical zeros
============================================================
  |Z_A(rho, tau)| / |Z_A(rho, 1)| at the first six zero heights
     tau | ratios at the first six zeros
       0 | 7.5394  5.7193  4.3529  6.8766  6.1449  3.4518
     0.5 | 2.2516  2.0748  1.9675  2.3530  2.1915  1.7530
       2 | 0.5511  0.5651  0.5505  0.5331  0.5432  0.5566
  The ratio is well defined (common truncation cancels). Two things
  make it informative despite the oscillation:
    - it is tightly clustered across the six zeros (spread ~2x), which
      a noisy quantity would not be;
    - it is NOT equal to 1 at the classical zeros. If the graded trace
      vanished there, the ratio would be far below 1. At tau=0 it is
      3.5 to 7.5; at tau=2 it is 0.53 to 0.57.
  The tau=1 control in shift_zeta_comparison.png shows the method does
  resolve zeros -- the trace there dips sharply exactly at the classical
  zeros, and the tau=0 panel shows no corresponding dips. Therefore the
  graded trace is strictly larger at those heights and its minima lie
  elsewhere: the zero sets do not coincide.

  This is numerical evidence, not a proof. What it establishes is that
  for tau != 1 the graded trace is not merely a scalar multiple of
  zeta; what it does not establish is any theorem about the critical
  line.

  A withdrawn statistic is recorded for honesty: 'deepest local minima'
  scored 7 of 8 hits at tau=1, the control, so it was measuring
  oscillation rather than zeros and is not used.
